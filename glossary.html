Fly Glossary
Flies on this page are organized by category and hatch group. Use this at the fly shop counter to identify patterns. Flies that cover multiple hatches are noted.

Spinners
Spent adult mayflies that have mated and fallen on the surface with wings flat. Often the most important and most overlooked stage — especially at dusk.

Attractor Patterns
These flies don't imitate a specific insect but trigger strikes through flash, color, movement, or a general buggy appearance. Many are local guide favorites — especially on stocked and Delayed Harvest water where fish respond to presentation and flash more than exact imitation. Due South Outfitters recommends several of these regularly in their fishing reports.

Streamers
Larger subsurface flies that imitate baitfish, leeches, crayfish, or other big prey. Stripped or swung through the water with an active retrieve. Use streamers in high or off-color water, cold water early/late season, or any time you want to target the biggest fish in the hole. Effective in both rivers and stillwater — Lake Coffey and Buckeye Reservoir fish streamers well.
Woolly Buggers
Woolly Bugger (Black)
The most versatile streamer ever tied — imitates leeches, baitfish, large nymphs. Strip it, swing it, or dead-drift it. Works everywhere.
Woolly Bugger (Olive)
Olive variant — deadly on stillwater like Lake Coffey when damselflies are active. Also a strong choice in off-color river water.
